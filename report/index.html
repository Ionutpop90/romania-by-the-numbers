Romania by the Numbers: where it leads Europe, where it lags, and where it is heading

Romania is often described in extremes: the EU’s fastest-growing economy, or the bottom of every league table. Both are true, just in different areas. I ranked Romania against the other 26 member states on 38 official indicators in seven domains, once around 2014 and once at the latest data point, to see what it is good at, what it is bad at, and which way each is moving. The same model ranks every EU country: you can switch the main charts to any of them.

      05 · People

      Fewer people, fewer births, and emigration that has turned around

      Romania has been losing population for decades. Since 2022 the balance of migration has turned positive, with more people arriving than leaving, but deaths now exceed births by a wider margin and fertility has fallen sharply. These context series are not ranked: there is no obvious “better” direction.

        06 · Methodology

        How the benchmark works

        One catalogue file defines all 38 indicators. Changing it and re-running two commands rebuilds every ranking.

        Catalogue38 indicators · direction · filters
        →
        ExtractEurostat API · 40 datasets
        →
        RankDuckDB window functions
        →
        Testcoverage rule · breaks · bootstrap
        →
        PublishSVG/JS report

        Ranking rules

        • Rank 1 = best, using each indicator’s policy direction (e.g. lower is better for poverty, higher for employment). Directions follow the EU’s SDG monitoring framework.
        • Ranks are computed only in years when at least 24 of 27 countries report, so ranks are comparable over time.
        • Start = first such year from 2014 (2015, 2019 or 2021 for newer series). Latest = the country’s most recent ranked year.
        • Tiers: top third = rank 1–9, bottom third = 19–27. Momentum: climbing/falling = moved at least 3 places.
        • Overall score = mean of per-indicator scores, where score = 1 − (rank − 1)/(n − 1).

        Data quality

        • Inverted code caught: Eurostat’s I_IUX in the internet-use table means “never used the internet”. The catalogue uses I_IU3 (used in the last 3 months).
        • Definition change: the e-government indicator changed in 2022 (I_IUGOV12 → I_IUGOV1). The series is spliced and compared through ranks, which survive the break because all countries switched together.
        • EU-wide survey breaks (labour force survey 2021) affect all countries at once, so ranks stay comparable.
        • One aggregate (greenhouse gases per person) has no EU-27 value in Eurostat and shows as n/a.

        Limitations

        • The choice of indicators shapes any composite ranking. The bootstrap tests this, and Romania’s rank stays between 25 and 27.
        • Ranks show position, not size of gap: rank 27 can be close to 26 or far from it. The trend charts show values.
